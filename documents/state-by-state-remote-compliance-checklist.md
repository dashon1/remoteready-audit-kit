# State-by-State Remote Work Compliance Checklist

**RemoteReady Audit Kit — 2026 Edition**

This checklist covers the 10 US states with the highest concentrations of remote workers. For each state, review the requirements that apply when you have an employee working remotely from that state — regardless of where your company is headquartered.

> **Important:** This checklist reflects the regulatory landscape as of early 2026 and is intended as a practical reference, not legal advice. Laws in this area change frequently. Verify current requirements with a licensed employment attorney in each relevant state before making compliance decisions.

---

## How to Use This Checklist

For each state where you have a remote employee, work through that state's section and check each item. An unchecked box means a potential compliance gap requiring action. Items marked **(Pro Kit)** include detailed guidance and template language in the full Pro Kit materials.

## 1. California

California has the most expansive employee protections of any US state. If you have a single remote employee in California, California law applies to that employee regardless of where your company is based.

**Labor Law Differences**
- [ ] California does not recognize "at-will" employment the same way most states do — verify your employment agreements are California-compliant
- [ ] California requires meal breaks (30 min) for shifts over 5 hours and 10-minute rest breaks for every 4 hours worked — remote workers are entitled to the same breaks as office workers
- [ ] California overtime kicks in after 8 hours/day (not just 40 hours/week) — confirm your timesheet system captures daily hours for CA employees
- [ ] California's WARN Act threshold is 75 employees statewide — factor in CA remote workers when calculating headcount

**Tax Nexus**
- [ ] A single California-based remote employee likely creates California income tax nexus for your company
- [ ] Register with the California Franchise Tax Board and Employment Development Department (EDD) if not already done
- [ ] Withhold California state income tax from CA employee's wages
- [ ] Pay California employer payroll taxes including CA SUI (State Unemployment Insurance) and SDI (State Disability Insurance)

**Required Employee Notices**
- [ ] Provide California WARN Act notice requirements disclosure (if 75+ employees)
- [ ] Provide notice of California SDI and PFL (Paid Family Leave) program rights at hire
- [ ] Provide California Rights Under CFRA (California Family Rights Act) notice
- [ ] Include California-specific statements in offer letters and employee handbooks

**Expense Reimbursement**
- [ ] California Labor Code Section 2802 requires employers to reimburse ALL necessary business expenses — this includes internet, phone, home office equipment, and any other expense required to perform the job
- [ ] "Necessary" is broadly interpreted in California — when in doubt, reimburse it
- [ ] There is no cap on what must be reimbursed under CA 2802 — an employee can sue for the full cost of any unreimbursed necessary expense

## 2. Texas

Texas is a generally employer-friendly state with fewer mandatory employee protections than most. However, remote employees in Texas still create tax and registration obligations.

**Labor Law Differences**
- [ ] Texas follows federal minimum wage ($7.25/hr) — no state minimum wage above federal
- [ ] Texas has no state income tax — no state income tax withholding required
- [ ] Texas is an at-will employment state with broad employer discretion
- [ ] Texas does not have a statewide paid sick leave law (note: Austin and Dallas have attempted local ordinances; verify current status)

**Tax Nexus**
- [ ] Texas remote employees likely create Texas franchise tax nexus — determine if your company must file Texas franchise tax returns
- [ ] Register with the Texas Workforce Commission (TWC) for unemployment insurance purposes
- [ ] Pay Texas employer SUI (State Unemployment Insurance) contributions
- [ ] Texas has no personal state income tax — no state income tax withholding from employees

**Required Employee Notices**
- [ ] Provide Texas Payday Law notice to employees (pay frequency, pay date, method)
- [ ] Post or provide required Texas workplace posters/notices (available from TWC)
- [ ] Provide workers' compensation notice if your company carries TX workers' comp (or notice that you are a non-subscriber)

**Expense Reimbursement**
- [ ] Texas does not have a statute requiring expense reimbursement beyond preventing wages from falling below minimum wage
- [ ] Reimbursement is still a best practice and may be required by contract or offer letter language
- [ ] Document any expense reimbursement agreements in writing

## 3. New York

New York has robust employee protections and active enforcement. New York City adds an additional layer of requirements beyond state law.

**Labor Law Differences**
- [ ] New York minimum wage varies by region: NYC/Long Island/Westchester vs. remainder of state — confirm which rate applies to your employee's work location
- [ ] New York Paid Family Leave (NYPFL) — employer must deduct PFL contributions from employee wages and provide PFL benefits
- [ ] New York requires written notice of pay rate, pay day, and paystub format at hire
- [ ] New York City: if employee works in NYC, NYC Human Rights Law applies (broader protections than state law)
- [ ] New York Earned Safe and Sick Time Act — employees accrue paid sick leave; confirm accrual tracking for remote workers

**Tax Nexus**
- [ ] A New York remote employee almost certainly creates NY income tax nexus
- [ ] Register with New York State Department of Taxation and Finance
- [ ] Register with New York Department of Labor for UI purposes
- [ ] New York's "Convenience of the Employer" rule: NY can tax employees who work remotely for their own convenience rather than employer necessity — consult a tax advisor on this rule specifically

**Required Employee Notices**
- [ ] Provide New York Wage Theft Prevention Act (WTPA) notice at hire — must include pay rate, pay basis, regular payday, overtime rate, and employer contact info
- [ ] Provide New York State Department of Labor required workplace posters
- [ ] Provide NY PFL rights notice at hire

**Expense Reimbursement**
- [ ] New York does not have a broad expense reimbursement statute comparable to California
- [ ] Employers cannot make deductions from wages that would reduce pay below minimum wage
- [ ] NYC employers: consult NYC-specific guidance on reimbursement obligations

## 4. Florida

Florida is an employer-friendly, low-regulation state. No state income tax and fewer mandatory employee protections than most large states.

**Labor Law Differences**
- [ ] Florida minimum wage increases annually by constitutional amendment — confirm current rate (as of 2026: $14/hr, phasing toward $15)
- [ ] Florida has no state income tax
- [ ] Florida is an at-will employment state
- [ ] Florida does not have a statewide paid sick leave or family leave law beyond federal FMLA

**Tax Nexus**
- [ ] Florida remote employees likely create Florida corporate income tax nexus
- [ ] Register with Florida Department of Revenue for corporate income tax if applicable
- [ ] Register with Florida Department of Economic Opportunity for SUI
- [ ] Pay Florida employer SUI contributions

**Required Employee Notices**
- [ ] Provide required Florida workplace postings (available from FL Dept. of Economic Opportunity)
- [ ] Provide Florida Workers' Compensation notice
- [ ] No state-specific income tax withholding form required (no state income tax)

**Expense Reimbursement**
- [ ] Florida has no statute requiring expense reimbursement
- [ ] Reimburse per company policy and contractual obligations
- [ ] Document reimbursement agreements to avoid wage deduction disputes

## 5. Washington State

Washington has strong employee protections, a high minimum wage, and significant paid leave requirements. Note: Washington State law is distinct from Washington D.C.

**Labor Law Differences**
- [ ] Washington minimum wage is among the highest in the nation — confirm current rate (indexed annually; $16.66/hr in 2024, increases each year)
- [ ] Washington Paid Family and Medical Leave (PFML) — mandatory state program; both employer and employee pay premiums; register and remit premiums
- [ ] Washington requires meal and rest breaks consistent with state L&I rules
- [ ] Washington has no state income tax

**Tax Nexus**
- [ ] Washington has Business & Occupation (B&O) tax based on gross receipts — a remote employee may create B&O nexus
- [ ] Register with Washington Department of Revenue for B&O tax
- [ ] Register with Washington Employment Security Department for SUI and PFML

**Required Employee Notices**
- [ ] Provide Washington PFML employee notice at hire
- [ ] Provide Washington L&I required workplace posters
- [ ] Provide Washington Workers' Compensation information

**Expense Reimbursement**
- [ ] Washington does not have a broad expense reimbursement statute
- [ ] Employers must not deduct expenses that would reduce wages below minimum wage
- [ ] Best practice: reimburse internet and equipment costs for dedicated remote workers

## 6. Illinois

Illinois, particularly Cook County and Chicago, has extensive employee protections. Chicago adds significant additional requirements.

**Labor Law Differences**
- [ ] Illinois minimum wage: $15/hr statewide; Chicago minimum wage is higher — confirm applicable rate
- [ ] Illinois Employee Sick Leave Act and Chicago Paid Sick Leave Ordinance — remote workers in Illinois/Chicago accrue paid sick leave
- [ ] Illinois One Day Rest In Seven Act requires at least 24 consecutive hours off per week
- [ ] Illinois Biometric Information Privacy Act (BIPA): if your tools use any biometric identifiers (face ID, fingerprint time clocks), Illinois has extremely strict requirements and a private right of action

**Tax Nexus**
- [ ] Illinois remote employee creates IL income tax nexus
- [ ] Register with Illinois Department of Revenue
- [ ] Register with Illinois Department of Employment Security for SUI
- [ ] Withhold Illinois state income tax (flat rate)

**Required Employee Notices**
- [ ] Provide required Illinois workplace postings
- [ ] Provide Chicago Paid Sick Leave and Paid Leave notice (if employee is in Chicago)
- [ ] Provide Illinois Employee Classification Act notice if engaging contractors

**Expense Reimbursement**
- [ ] Illinois Wage Payment and Collection Act (IWPCA) Section 9.5 requires employers to reimburse "all necessary expenditures or losses" incurred by employees in direct consequence of the discharge of their duties — similar strength to California
- [ ] Internet, phone, and equipment expenses for remote work are reimbursable under IWPCA
- [ ] Keep expense reimbursement records for at least 3 years

## 7. Colorado

Colorado has enacted several significant employee protection laws in recent years and is one of the more complex states for remote work compliance.

**Labor Law Differences**
- [ ] Colorado COMPS Order sets overtime, break, and minimum wage rules — applies to most private employers
- [ ] Colorado Healthy Families and Workplaces Act: up to 48 hours paid sick leave per year
- [ ] Colorado FAMLI (Family and Medical Leave Insurance) — mandatory state program launching in 2024/2025; both employer and employee contribute premiums
- [ ] Colorado requires written notice of employee rights under COMPS Order at hire and must post COMPS Order notice

**Tax Nexus**
- [ ] Colorado remote employee creates CO income tax nexus
- [ ] Register with Colorado Department of Revenue
- [ ] Register with Colorado Department of Labor and Employment for SUI and FAMLI

**Required Employee Notices**
- [ ] Provide COMPS Order 2024 poster/notice to all employees (including remote)
- [ ] Provide Colorado FAMLI notice at hire
- [ ] Provide Colorado Healthy Families and Workplaces Act sick leave notice

**Expense Reimbursement**
- [ ] Colorado COMPS Order requires employers to reimburse employees for all necessary business expenses
- [ ] Remote work tools and equipment needed to perform the job qualify as necessary expenses
- [ ] Submit reimbursements within 30 days of expense submission

## 8. Massachusetts

Massachusetts has strong worker protections and complex wage payment rules.

**Labor Law Differences**
- [ ] Massachusetts Earned Sick Time Law: employees earn 1 hour of sick time per 30 hours worked
- [ ] Massachusetts Blue Laws govern Sunday and holiday work — some industries have restrictions; verify for your sector
- [ ] Massachusetts strict rules on independent contractor classification (3-part ABC test) — harder to classify workers as contractors in MA
- [ ] Massachusetts requires weekly pay for certain employee classes — confirm pay frequency compliance

**Tax Nexus**
- [ ] Massachusetts remote employee creates MA income tax nexus
- [ ] Register with Massachusetts Department of Revenue
- [ ] Register with Massachusetts Department of Unemployment Assistance
- [ ] Massachusetts has a Paid Family and Medical Leave (PFML) program — register and remit contributions

**Required Employee Notices**
- [ ] Provide Massachusetts PFML notice at hire
- [ ] Provide Massachusetts Earned Sick Time notice
- [ ] Provide required MA workplace posters

**Expense Reimbursement**
- [ ] Massachusetts does not have a specific expense reimbursement statute at the level of California or Illinois
- [ ] Wage deductions that reduce pay below minimum wage are prohibited
- [ ] Best practice: written reimbursement policy documenting all covered expenses

## 9. Georgia

Georgia is a business-friendly state with relatively modest compliance requirements, but federal protections fully apply.

**Labor Law Differences**
- [ ] Georgia minimum wage is $5.15/hr (state) but federal minimum wage of $7.25 controls
- [ ] Georgia is an at-will employment state
- [ ] No state paid sick leave, family leave, or local ordinance requirements (as of 2026)
- [ ] Georgia follows federal FMLA for eligible employers

**Tax Nexus**
- [ ] Georgia remote employee creates GA income tax nexus
- [ ] Register with Georgia Department of Revenue
- [ ] Register with Georgia Department of Labor for SUI

**Required Employee Notices**
- [ ] Provide required Georgia workplace postings (Workers' Compensation, Unemployment Insurance)
- [ ] Georgia employers must post Georgia Department of Labor posters

**Expense Reimbursement**
- [ ] No Georgia-specific expense reimbursement statute
- [ ] Reimburse per company policy; document agreements in writing

## 10. Arizona

Arizona has a growing remote workforce and moderate compliance requirements relative to the West Coast states.

**Labor Law Differences**
- [ ] Arizona minimum wage increases annually (indexed to inflation); confirm current year rate
- [ ] Arizona Earned Paid Sick Time: employees accrue paid sick time (up to 40 hours/year for employers with 15+ employees; up to 24 hours/year for smaller employers)
- [ ] Arizona has no state family and medical leave law beyond federal FMLA
- [ ] Arizona does not require daily overtime (only weekly, following federal rules)

**Tax Nexus**
- [ ] Arizona remote employee creates AZ income tax nexus
- [ ] Register with Arizona Department of Revenue
- [ ] Register with Arizona Department of Economic Security for SUI
- [ ] Withhold Arizona state income tax

**Required Employee Notices**
- [ ] Provide Arizona Earned Paid Sick Time notice at hire
- [ ] Provide required Arizona Industrial Commission workplace posters
- [ ] Provide Workers' Compensation coverage notice

**Expense Reimbursement**
- [ ] No Arizona-specific expense reimbursement statute
- [ ] Employers must not make wage deductions that reduce pay below minimum wage
- [ ] Document reimbursement policy in writing; cover internet and equipment costs as best practice
